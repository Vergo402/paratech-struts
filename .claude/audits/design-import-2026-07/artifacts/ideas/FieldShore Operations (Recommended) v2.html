Why this leads on the phone

Two zones, not three. "Needs you" — the 1–2 points wanting your hands, each with a button — sits above "On the floor," everything underway. The groups look different, so the split needs no explaining; the guess-what-the-bucket-means confusion is gone.

It looks built, not "simplest." Considered type, real spacing, status edges, and tabular instrument numbers — the craft is what separates this from a vibe-coded list.

Nobody bounces. A rookie firefighter and a battalion chief both already know how to read a grouped list under stress. Lowest training, highest trust.
